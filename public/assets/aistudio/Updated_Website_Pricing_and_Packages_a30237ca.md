## 2. Business Card Web Page — R1,580

**Best for:** A simple online presence that gives customers the business's key details.

- One image-led page / digital business-card layout.
- Business name, contact details and supplied links.
- Clear call-to-action based on the agreed brief.

**Important scope note:** If this is delivered as a fixed image or static visual rather than a fully built responsive website, label it clearly as a *digital business-card page/design*. Do not promise responsive layouts, editable content, forms or interactive features unless they are included and tested.

## 3. Three-page custom website

### Essentials — R6,500
**Best for:** Sole traders, contractors and small businesses that need a clean, affordable web presence.

- Three pages: Home, About and Contact.
- One agreed design direction.
- Mobile-responsive layout.
- Basic on-page SEO: page titles and meta descriptions.
- Contact form that sends enquiries to the agreed email address.
- Google Maps embed and social-media links.
- One round of consolidated revisions.
- Basic self-help handover guide.

**Not included:** WhatsApp quick chat, custom animations, video gallery, live training or extra pages. WhatsApp can be added for R500.

### Professional — R8,980
**Best for:** Businesses that want stronger engagement and a more polished presentation.

Everything in Essentials, plus:
- WhatsApp quick-chat button.
- Animated hero/banner treatment where suitable.
- Image gallery and embedded YouTube/Vimeo video section.
- Standard scroll animations where they support usability.
- Two rounds of consolidated revisions.
- One 30-minute editing/handover session.

**Customer-facing explanation:** “Essentials gets your business online with the important information. Professional adds more ways for visitors to engage with your business and gives the site a more polished presentation.”

## 12. Standard terms to include in every quotation

1. **Currency and VAT:** Prices are in South African rand. State whether VAT is applicable and whether listed prices are VAT-inclusive or VAT-exclusive. Do not describe a price as excluding VAT unless that is your actual tax treatment.
2. **Deposit and milestones:** Suggested starting point: 50% deposit, 30% after design approval, and 20% before launch/handover. Adjust for project size and risk.
3. **Client responsibilities:** Client supplies accurate business details, approved copy, images, product data, legal policies and access credentials by agreed deadlines.
4. **Revisions:** A revision round means one consolidated list of changes. New pages, new features or changes to an approved direction are charged separately.
5. **Third-party costs:** Domain, hosting, email, paid plugins, payment processing, courier services, AI/API usage, CRM and email-marketing subscriptions are paid by the client unless explicitly included.
6. **Payment and checkout:** Payment providers control approval, fees, settlement and compliance. Their terms can change.
7. **SEO and outcomes:** Basic SEO setup does not guarantee rankings, traffic, enquiries or sales.
8. **Support:** State the support period, response times, what counts as a bug, and what counts as new work. Third-party outages and client changes are not automatically free fixes.
9. **Security and backups:** State which backups, updates, monitoring and recovery responsibilities are included.
10. **Ownership and handover:** Define when the client receives source code, admin access, design assets and account ownership, and identify any third-party/licensed components.
11. **Acceptance and launch:** Define how the client approves the site and what testing is included before launch.
12. **Change requests:** Any work outside the agreed scope requires a written quote and approval.

## 13. Recommended improvements before publishing

- **Use feature-based comparisons, not only page counts.** The tier names should make it clear why the higher tier costs more.
- **Keep package boundaries consistent.** Decide whether “two design concepts” means two initial visual directions or two complete website designs. For most projects, two design directions plus two revision rounds is clearer.
- **Define page counts.** Say whether privacy policy, terms, blog posts, product pages and landing pages count toward the page limit.
- **Be precise about responsiveness.** Reserve “mobile-responsive website” for a real layout that adapts to different screens; do not use it for a single fixed-size image.
- **Treat SEO claims carefully.** Sitemaps and structured data can support search visibility but do not guarantee higher rankings.
- **Do not promise that animations or sliders convert better.** Offer them as presentation features and keep performance/accessibility in mind.
- **Clarify support.** “30 days support” should say whether it means bug fixes only, how clients report issues, and whether content edits are included.
- **Protect low-priced packages.** At R6,500, limit custom functionality and require supplied content. Use paid change requests for work outside the package.
- **Check the 9-page and catalogue upsells.** The R1,420 uplift from Corporate Presence to Marketing Hub and R1,780 uplift from Digital Showroom to Interactive Catalogue may be too small if blog, newsletter, structured data and support require substantial work.
- **Validate platform feasibility before promising features.** Customer accounts, shipping calculators, product variations, AI recommendations and courier APIs depend on the selected platform and provider.
- **Separate setup from ongoing operations.** A one-time build fee does not automatically include hosting, updates, content management, marketing, AI usage or ongoing support.
- **Keep a private cost sheet.** Estimate hours, contractor costs, hosting/API costs, contingency and target margin before finalising each price.
